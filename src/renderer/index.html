        And thank you for the software
        
          
        These are the licenses on the pieces Nova Collar is built with, and what each one asks of anyone who keeps a copy.
        
          
        MIT
        
          
        Electron 36, xterm.js and its fit addon, node-pty, and qrcode are MIT. electron-builder and @electron/rebuild, the tools that package the window, are MIT too.
        
          
        The MIT license lets us use, change, and share them. The duty is to keep the copyright line and the permission notice with any real copy of that software. It is offered as is, with no warranty.
        
          
        Electron’s notice names the Electron contributors and GitHub Inc. xterm.js names the xterm.js authors, SourceLair, and Christopher Jeffrey. node-pty’s notice names Christopher Jeffrey. qrcode names Ryan Day.
        
          
        Chromium, inside Electron
        
          
        Electron ships Chromium. Chromium’s own notices travel in the Electron distribution, in LICENSES.chromium.html. The BSD pieces ask that the copyright, the conditions, and the disclaimer stay with the software. Their names are not a stamp of approval for Nova Collar.
        
          
        Apache 2.0
        
          
        Laya, the small decision model, starts from convaiinnovations/laya on Hugging Face. That model card is Apache 2.0. Thank you to Convai Innovations for releasing it.
        
          
        The checkpoint in models/laya-np is a modified copy. We trained it on Nova Collar’s job, loop, goal, and audit questions. Apache 2.0 asks us to keep their license, say that the weights were changed, and retain their attribution notices. There is no warranty, and their trademarks are still theirs.
        
          
        Ollama
        
          
        If setup installs Ollama, that project is MIT as well. Same duty: keep its copyright and permission notice with the copy. Nova Collar does not ship Ollama inside the window.
